The Navier-Stokes equations model the behaviour of viscous incompressible fluids. The question of whether or not the Navier-Stokes equations, or the inviscid Euler equations, are a valid description of fluid mechanics remains unanswered. A singularity is the occurrence of an infinite derivative of the velocity at a single point in the fluid flow. If the flow forms a singularity from smooth initial conditions, then the equations are not a valid descriptions of fluid mechanics. The Euler-Voigt equations are an inviscid regularization of the Euler equations which are known to not form singularities from smooth initial conditions. However, by decreasing the regularization parameter we approximate Euler flows. We examine the Euler-Voigt equations with a PDE-constrained optimization problem to identify smooth initial conditions that produce 'extreme' behaviour in numerical simulations. {\color{red}We present numerical evidence that, upon optimization, singularities can form in Euler flows corresponding to smooth initial conditions.}
