\chapter{Derivation of the G\^{a}teaux Directional Differential of the Objective Functional}
\label{app:gateaux}

In this Appendix we derive an expression for the G\^{a}teaux directional differential of the objective functional, $\ppat(\bea;\bepa)$ in terms of the $\hdo$ inner product and $L^2$ inner product. This differential is used to obtain the gradient of the objective functional.
\\~\\
We begin by substituting the second definition of (\ref{eq:objective:functional}) into (\ref{eq:Gateaux:differential}) to obtain
$$\ppat(\bea;\bepa)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}[\|\ua(T;\bea+\epsilon\bepa)\|_{\hdo}^2-\|\ua(T;\bea)\|_{\hdo}^2].$$
Using the $\hdo$ seminorm as defined in Appedix \ref{app:function:spaces}, and taking the limit $\epsilon\rightarrow 0$, we can express $\ppat$ in the form of the $\hdo$ inner product
$$\ppat(\bea;\bepa)=  2\la\ua(T),\upa(T)\ra_{\hdo}.$$
Using the integral form of the $\dot{H}^s$ seminorm from Appendix \ref{app:function:spaces}, the G\^{a}teaux differential can be expressed in physical space as
$$\ppat(\bea;\bepa)= \int_{\TT^3}2|D|^2\ua(\x,T)\cdot\upa(\x,T)\,d\x.$$
We now make use of the terminal condition in the adjoint system imposed in Appendix \ref{app:adjoint}
$$\usa(x,T) = 2|D|^2\ua(\x,T).$$
Therefore,
$$\ppat(\bea;\bepa) = \int_{\TT^3}\usa(\x,T)\cdot\upa(\x,T)\,d\x.$$
Now using the duality-pairing \ref{eq:duality:pairing}
$$\ppat(\bea;\bepa) = \int_{\TT^3}\usa(\x,0)\cdot\bepa(\x)\,d\x,$$
which lets us express the value of $\ppat$ with the $L^2$ inner product
$$\ppat(\bea;\bepa) = \la \usa(0), \bepa\ra_{L^2}.$$
Therefore,
$$\ppat(\bea;\bepa) = 2\la\ua(T),\upa(T)\ra_{\hdo} = \la \usa(0), \bepa\ra_{L^2}.$$
As described in Section \ref{sec:Gateaux Directional Differential}, the differential can also be expressed as a Gevrey inner product using the Riesz representation theorem (\ref{eq:Gateaux:differential:riesz}). Using the definition of the Gevrey inner product (\ref{eq:Gevrey:Inner:Product}), the $L^2$ inner product can be converted to the Gevrey inner product,
$$\la \usa(0),\upa(0)\ra_{L^2} = \la (1+|D|^2)^{-3}e^{-2\sigma|D|} \usa(0),\bepa \ra_{G^\sigma}.$$
Therefore, we have two forms of the G\^{a}teaux differential as a Gevrey inner product
$$\ppat(\bea;\bea') = \la \nabla\pat(\bea),\bepa \ra_{G^\sigma} = \la (1+|D|^2)^{-3}e^{-2\sigma|D|} \usa(0),\bepa \ra_{G^\sigma}.$$
From this we obtain the gradient of the objective functional
$$\nabla \pat(\bea) = (1+|D|^2)^{-3}e^{-2\sigma|D|}\usa(0).$$
